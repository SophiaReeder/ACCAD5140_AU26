WHAT ARE SERIOUS GAMES?

 
 

Serious games are a broad category that can span a range of genres, platforms, and purposes. Serious games is a term coined by Clark Abt in 1970, defined as “...purpose-driven playful environments intended to impact the players beyond the self-contained aim of the game. Serious game design is based on their purpose, their mechanics and rule-sets, fiction and narrative, their aesthetics and framing and their content” (Abt, 1970). It is important to compare and contrast serious games, simulation games, and digital game-based learning in order to understand the unique stance serious games take in the fields of education and design.

 
 

Few studies on serious games have looked at what knowledge is retained after a delayed period of time. Those that have learned that when comparing a game learning condition with a traditional learning condition, post-test scores remain similar in terms of instant recall, but through a delayed retention test, there is an increase in scores from the game condition (Luna et al., 2026, Wang et al., 2024). This indicates game designers should consider the variable of time in their work. This also pairs well with the concept of scaffolding, as time plays a factor in how much help the player is afforded in a given play session. What would happen if designers took into account knowledge retention theories, like repetitive and spaced out studying, and applied these concepts in an environment?
